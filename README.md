# swift-projection

Use this package when you need to map a view volume in three dimensions onto normalised coordinates: a perspective frustum, where things shrink with distance, or an orthographic box, where they do not. Start from `Projection<Scalar>`: give it the kind, the window on the near plane, the near and far distances and the depth range you want, then call `clip(_:)` for homogeneous clip coordinates, `normalized(_:)` for the point after the perspective divide, or `eye(_:)` to go back. `eye(_:distance:)` goes back from a normalised x and y at a known distance ahead of the eye, `Projection.Viewport` maps normalised coordinates onto the rectangle of an image and back, `radius(of:at:)` says how large a ball is drawn, and `interpolated(to:at:)` mixes two projections. It describes one projection and applies it to points; it holds no camera position or orientation. For the projection as a 4×4 matrix that multiplies with rotations and scales, enable the `Projection` trait of swift-matrix, which builds `Matrix<4, 4, Scalar>` from a `Projection`.

## Laws

- Eye space is right-handed and the eye looks along −z: x is right, y is up, and a point in front of the eye has negative z. `near` and `far` are distances along the view direction, so the near plane is z = −`near`. `left`, `right`, `bottom` and `top` bound the window on the near plane.
- Construction fails with `.nonfinite` when a bound is NaN or infinite, with `.empty` unless `left < right`, `bottom < top` and `near < far`, and with `.behindEye` when a perspective `near` is not positive. The one infinity allowed is a perspective `far` of `+infinity`. An orthographic `near` may be zero or negative. Decoding enforces the same rules.
- The view volume maps onto x and y in `-1...1` and depth in `depth.range`: `-1...1` for `.symmetric` and `0...1` for `.unit`. The near plane maps to the lower depth bound and the far plane to the upper; the window's left, right, bottom and top edges map to x = −1, x = 1, y = −1 and y = 1. Nothing is clipped: points outside the volume map outside those ranges.
- `clip(_:)` is the `coefficients` rows applied to (x, y, z, 1), each row summed from zero in column order. `coefficients` is row-major and treats points as columns, as swift-matrix does.
- In a perspective projection the fourth clip coordinate is −z, the distance in front of the eye, and points on one line through the eye share their normalised x and y. In an orthographic projection it is always 1, the map is affine, and x and y do not depend on z.
- `normalized(_:)` is the clip coordinates divided by the fourth, and nil when the fourth is zero, that is for a perspective point in the plane of the eye. A perspective point behind the eye has a negative fourth coordinate and is returned as the projective map gives it, mirrored.
- `eye(_:)` inverts `normalized(_:)`: `eye(normalized(p))` is `p` up to rounding. It is nil for a normalised point with no finite preimage, as at the upper depth bound of a projection with an infinite far plane.
- With an infinite far plane, depth increases with distance and stays below the upper bound.
- `perspective(fieldOfView:aspect:near:far:depth:)` centres the window on the view axis, with half height `near · tan(fieldOfView / 2)` and half width that times `aspect`. `orthographic(width:height:near:far:depth:)` centres a window of that width and height. Both then apply the construction rules, so a field of view of zero is `.empty`.
- `focal(fieldOfView:height:)` is `height / 2 / tan(fieldOfView / 2)`: the distance at which a window of that height fills the vertical field of view, in the unit of `height`. With the viewport's height in pixels it is the focal length in pixels, so a length `l` at distance `d` in front of the eye spans `l · focal / d` pixels of a centred perspective of that field of view. `field(focal:height:)` is its inverse, `2 · atan(height / 2 / focal)`, up to rounding, so a perspective that shows `n` pixels per unit at distance `d` is `perspective(fieldOfView: field(focal: n · d, height: height), …)`.
- `eye(_:distance:)` is the eye-space point at z = −distance whose normalised x and y are the ones given, so `normalized(eye(n, distance: d))` has x and y `n`, and the x and y of `normalized(p)` at distance −`p.z` give `p`, up to rounding. It needs no depth range. In a perspective projection a negative distance gives the mirrored point behind the eye, and a distance of zero the eye itself, which projects nowhere; in an orthographic projection x and y do not depend on the distance.
- A `Projection.Viewport` is a rectangle of an image: its left edge `x`, its least `y`, a positive finite `width` and `height`, and the `direction` the image's y grows. Construction fails with `.nonfinite` or `.empty` as a projection's does, and decoding enforces the same. `point(_:)` takes normalised x from −1 to 1 onto `x…x + width`, and normalised y from −1 to 1 onto the height so that y = 1 lands on the least y when the direction is `.down`, as a raster's rows run, and on the greatest when it is `.up`. `normalized(_:)` is its inverse, up to rounding. Both are affine and clip nothing. Depth is not the viewport's: carry the normalised z alongside.
- `radius(of:at:)` is how large a ball of a radius about an eye-space centre is drawn, in normalised units of y: half the width of its outline across the line from the view axis. In a perspective projection the outline is the exact silhouette, an ellipse drawn out along that line, and the half width across is `c11 · radius / √(z² − radius²)`, `c11` being `coefficients[1][1]`; that is `c11 · sin α / √(cos² θ − sin² α)` for a centre at distance d, θ off the axis and sin α = radius / d, and `c11 · tan α` on the axis. It is neither the half length along the line, which is longer, nor the small-angle `c11 · radius / −z`. It is nil when the ball reaches the plane of the eye, −z ≤ radius. In an orthographic projection it is `c11 · radius`. A negative or non-finite radius gives nil. The unit is vertical: the outline measures the same both ways in a viewport only when the viewport's aspect is the window's. `radius(of:at:in:)` is it times half the viewport's height.
- `interpolated(to:at:)` of two projections of the same kind and depth range moves each bound as `self · (1 − share) + other · share`, keeping a bound they share, so 0 gives this projection and 1 the other exactly, every share between gives a valid projection, and an infinite far plane mixed with a finite one stays infinite. Linear bounds do not turn the field of view at an even rate. Projections of different kinds or depth ranges, and shares beyond 0 to 1 whose bounds make no view volume, do not mix: the result is this projection below a share of 1 and the other from 1 on.
- Equality and hashing compare the stored bounds, kind and depth range, and of a viewport its rectangle and direction.
